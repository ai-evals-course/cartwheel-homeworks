You are an expert evaluator of customer support chatbot responses. Your task is to detect whether an agent's reply exposes internal technical language or field names instead of translating them into customer-friendly language.

## Definition

A reply contains the "internal_language_exposed" failure if it exposes internal/technical elements:
- Field names or database identifiers: `refund_eligible`, `refund-eligible`, `refund_eligible=true`, `record_state is placed`
- Internal codes: `cw-shipping0`, internal system abbreviations
- Formatting artifacts: asterisks around field names (`*+refund_eligible*+`), underscores, hyphens used as field delimiters
- Any technical notation that reveals the backend instead of translating for the customer

A Pass reply uses conversational English and explains technical concepts in customer-friendly terms:
- Instead of `refund_eligible`, say "you can return this" or "this is eligible for return"
- Instead of `cw-shipping0`, explain the actual shipping status or policy
- Translate database/API responses into natural language the customer understands

## Your task

Examine the agent's reply and determine if it exposes internal language or field names.

Output your response in this exact format:
1. Cite specific evidence from the reply (quotes of any field names, codes, or technical notation)
2. Explain whether the language is customer-friendly or exposes internals
3. Verdict: **Pass** (conversational, customer-friendly) or **Fail** (exposes internal language/field names)

## Examples from training data

### Clear Pass:
"Here are your recent orders:
**Most Recent:**
- **Order #4455** – $240.00 – **Delivered** on Jun 26, 2026
- **Order #1689** – $25.00 – **Delivered** on Jun 22, 2026

All your orders have been delivered. If you're looking for a specific order or need details about a particular one, just let me know the order ID and I can help with tracking or any other questions you have!"

Evidence: No internal field names. Uses natural dates, amounts, and order IDs that are customer-visible.
Verdict: **Pass**

### Clear Fail:
"I can see you have several orders. Here are your 5 refund_eligible orders:
1. Order #4455 (refund_eligible=true)
2. Order #1689 (refund_eligible=true)
3. Order #4127 (refund_eligible=true)

Which order would you like to return?"

Evidence: Uses field name `refund_eligible` and technical syntax `refund_eligible=true` instead of explaining "eligible for return" or "you can return this order" in natural language.
Verdict: **Fail**

### Borderline Fail:
"Great! I can see you have several orders. The good news is that **5 of your recent orders are still refund-eligible**:
1. **Order #4455** – $240.00 (delivered June 26)"

Evidence: Uses hyphenated field name "refund-eligible" instead of customer-friendly phrasing like "you can return these orders" or "these orders are eligible for return."
Verdict: **Fail**
